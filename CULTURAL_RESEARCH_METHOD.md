# Cultural research method

## Editorial principles

- Describe a named calendar, manuscript, community, or historical context rather than treating a continent or religion as one culture.
- Separate a documented cultural meaning or practice from physical lunar mechanisms. The application does not assert causation of political, economic, geological, ecological, or human events without evidence specific to that claim.
- Keep date conventions distinct: astronomical phase time, local civil date, crescent visibility, and a community's calendar rule are not interchangeable.
- Prefer primary materials and scholarly or community-authorized sources. Preserve scope qualifiers and uncertainty beside the record.
- Do not publish sacred, restricted, or sensitive Indigenous knowledge. Publicly available secondary references do not imply community endorsement.
- Avoid creating calendar conversion features until regional rules and date algorithms can be source-reviewed and tested.

## Current coverage

The bundled curated sample includes:

- South Asia: amanta and purnimanta month-boundary conventions in Hindu lunisolar calendars.
- East Asia: a general Chinese lunisolar month/intercalation description.
- West Asia and the Middle East: Islamic Hijri month-start variation, without issuing religious dates.
- Mesoamerica: scholarly interpretation of lunar tables in the Dresden Codex.
- Aotearoa New Zealand: public reference to maramataka with iwi/place variation.
- Ancient Europe: the Greek Metonic-cycle calendar concept.

These are brief reference notes, not a complete cultural atlas. Citations, confidence language, and limits are visible in each record.

## Gaps and next research steps

There are no currently bundled, specifically sourced records for African societies, Southeast Asia, Indigenous North American communities, Andean or Amazonian societies, Arctic communities, or most Pacific/Oceanian traditions. Their absence is a research gap, not evidence that lunar knowledge or practice is absent. Additions should be supported by locally specific scholarship or community-authorized public sources. Avoid extrapolating one group's practices to neighboring communities.

The dataset currently does not implement Hijri, Hindu, Chinese, or Māori date conversion. Calendar conventions can differ by authority, location, observation threshold, and historical era; the reference descriptions are educational only.
